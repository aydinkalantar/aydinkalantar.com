01  Overview

Virtual Fashion is a browser studio for designing apparel and producing the imagery to sell it. A designer picks a garment, places artwork on it, casts a model, sets the scene and exports studio-quality photos and video, all in one workspace.

02  Challenges

  • Photography is the bottleneck. Convincing apparel imagery needs samples, a photographer, models and a location, or 3D software with a steep learning curve. Small brands and creators need it before a garment physically exists.
  • A prompt box is the wrong tool for a designer. Generative AI can make the pictures, but typing descriptions gives no direct control over where a logo sits or how a print wraps.
  • The same face, every time. A brand needs one model to look identical across every photo and video, which generative models do not do on their own.
  • Rendering is slow and costly. High-resolution images and video take minutes and real compute, so waiting on a spinner would stall the creative work.

03  UI/UX solutions

I organized the product around how a real shoot is planned: the product, the model, the set, then the motion. That sequence became the navigation itself: Workspace, Identity, Motion and Showcase.

A smart canvas, not a prompt

The garment stays at the center of the screen and the designer works on it directly. Logos are dropped on and placed automatically, one uploaded pattern repeats across the garment, and material finishes (embroidery, puff print, screen print, sublimation) are picked from a panel rather than described in words.

Identity as its own step

Casting is a dedicated screen with a library of lifestyle and runway models. Designers can also cast their own face from a single selfie, or upload a full-body photo for a personal try-on, so consistency is a choice made once instead of a prompt detail repeated every time.

One-click scenes and motion

Nine scene presets, from studio gray to a golden-hour desert, switch with one tap. A short instruction makes the model walk, pose or spin, with the output format (9:16 for Reels and TikTok, 16:9 for lookbooks) chosen up front.

Never make people wait

Renders run in the background while design continues, and land in a private gallery. The same flow works on a phone, where the studio is one thumb away.

04  Technical solutions

A generation pipeline behind the canvas

Each canvas action becomes structured input for the image models: garment, artwork placement, material finish, model identity and scene. That keeps results predictable and repeatable, instead of depending on how a prompt happens to be worded.

Automatic artwork placement

PNG, JPG and SVG logos are positioned on the garment automatically, and uploaded patterns are tiled so the repeat follows the folds of the fabric rather than sitting flat on top.

Identity that stays consistent

A model's identity is stored once and reused in every image and video render, so the same face carries across a whole shoot. A single selfie is enough to create a new identity.

A cloud render queue

Image and video jobs run in the cloud and save to the user's private gallery. Video is generated with Kling 2.6 Pro, Kling 3.0 Pro or Veo 3.1, in 4, 6 or 8 second clips, directed by a preset motion style or a written camera direction, and the cost in credits is shown before anything starts. Batch Mode queues large runs at half the credit cost and emails the user when they finish. Exports cover PNG, WebP and MP4 at 2K for images and 4K for video.

One credit system

Images and video draw from a single credit balance across Starter, Creator and Agency plans, with top-up packs and a referral loop that rewards both sides. The app is installable on phone and desktop.

05  Outcome

Virtual Fashion is live, with free starter credits for every new account.

  • Exports PNG, WebP and MP4, with 2K images and 4K video.
  • Full commercial rights for e-commerce, social and pitch decks.
  • One credit system across images and video, with Starter, Creator and Agency plans plus top-up packs.
  • A referral loop that rewards both sides with credits.
