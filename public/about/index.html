About

Designer, developer, maker.

I'm Aydin Kalantar, a product designer based in Mayfield Heights, Ohio. I take products from first sketch to shipped interface, and I build the code behind them. Before any of that, I learned precision at a workbench, cutting natural wood veneer by hand.

Story

My work sits where digital systems meet physical craft.

Marquetry taught me to see a whole picture as hundreds of small parts, each cut to fit its neighbors. A portrait in veneer only works when every fragment respects the grain, the tone and the line next to it. There is no undo.

I bring the same discipline to software. A product is a system of small decisions, from a button's state to a pricing rule, and it only feels effortless when each one fits. I design those decisions, then build them, so nothing gets lost between the idea and what ships.

Along the way I worked in graphic design and in 3D modeling and rendering. That is where I learned composition, light and material, and it still shapes how I present every product.

Today that practice runs in parallel: three live products on one side, original veneer artwork on the other.

My family name appears in a few spellings, so you may also find me as Aydin Kalantarov, Aydin Kalantarli or Aydin Kalan.

Introduction

A short hello.

Journey

From a waving flag in Baku to products in the browser.

  1. 1985

    Baku, Azerbaijan

    Born in Baku, the oldest of three children. As a kid I was obsessed with two things, art and computers, and I spent hours drawing on paper and then reworking the drawings on screen. My father and relatives saw something in that work and pushed me to study art.

  2. 1992

    A flag on a black screen

    When I was seven, my father’s friend and business partner showed me a flag he had coded, waving on a black screen. It was a simple piece of animation on a slow, difficult machine, and I knew nothing about programming, but it opened a door. From then on I wanted to make art and computers work together.

  3. 2001

    Art college

    I enrolled at the Azerbaijan Fine Art College in Baku and majored in oil painting. Alongside my studies I worked at my father’s printing company, where I learned Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop and carried what I learned at the easel into digital work. Experimenting with mixed media at college, I found marquetry, still one of my favorite techniques. See the marquetry.

  4. 2005

    3D for film

    Next came 3D: modeling, animation and rendering for film and television. My first CGI animation for a film was for The Temple’s Heart, directed by Teymur Daimi, in 2005.

  5. 2011

    Los Angeles

    My wife and I came to Los Angeles for our honeymoon and enrolled in a nine-month English program at Kaplan in Westwood. Our daughter was born here, and we decided to stay, in a country where I could say what I believe, in life and in my artwork.

  6. Next

    Learning business

    I was an artist, not a businessman, but I had a family to support. I built a relocation company from scratch and used my design background to give it a strong brand. On my father’s advice I took a three-day course in screen printing, learned the craft end to end, and started an apparel decoration business. Those years taught me what a design is worth only when it works for a real business.

  7. 2017–2020

    Brands for Los Angeles businesses

    Brand, website, SEO and paid advertising for The Best Screen Printing and Signs Market, described below.

  8. Today

    Mayfield Heights, Ohio

    Live products, Virtual Fashion, Catch Lead and Auto Alpha, and a rebuilt website for The Best Screen Printing on one side, and original veneer artwork on the other. The same idea that started with a waving flag: art and computers, made to work together.

Principles

  1. 01

    Respect the material.

    Wood has grain; data has shape; people have habits. Good work follows them instead of fighting them.

  2. 02

    Measure twice.

    Understand the problem before cutting anything. A clear plan is cheaper than a clever fix.

  3. 03

    Be honest in the interface.

    Show the risk beside the reward, the price before the commitment and the state of every action.

  4. 04

    Finish the edges.

    Empty states, errors and small screens are where craft shows. They get the same care as the hero.
